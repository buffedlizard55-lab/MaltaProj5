What the data shows — and what it cannot

  • It is a festival city with a thin weekly layer. 137 annual entries against 26 weekly ones. The weekly layer is mostly farmers markets, museum nights and standing park programmes.
  • Blank is the honest default. 119 rows carry review notes and 173 have at least one blank tracked field. Where an organizer has not published a next date, the date cell is blank rather than extrapolated — a recurring pattern is not a confirmed future instance.
  • A second, human pass changed things. Of the 70 rows re-read line by line against their official pages, 28 needed a correction — wrong venues (SF International Arts Festival, Queer Women of Color Film Festival, Peak2Peak), a festival whose two rows disagreed on its dates (Litquake), a fair that alternates cities (the Antiquarian Book Fair is in Los Angeles in 2027), an organizer site still showing last year's edition (Lowrider Parade), and renamed events (Superfair, The San Francisco Fall Show). Automated name-matching catches dead pages, not stale values.
  • Many entries record 2026 editions that have already taken place (for example Tulip Day in March, the Opera Ball gala and the July 4 fireworks). They are retained as dated records and are not presented as upcoming; the register has no “upcoming only” filter because that would require re-deriving dates the organizers have not published.
  • There is no citywide denominator. No official source publishes a complete count of San Francisco events, so this register cannot claim exhaustive coverage. Known gaps: library programmes, most theatre, neighbourhood block parties, sports beyond the Giants, and weekday civic meetings.
  • Two registers were reconciled. The 86-row register (sessions 3a19e72d/5a85baf2) and the 167-row inventory (session 4f47c142) were merged with an explicit map; overlapping rows were reconciled rather than counted twice.
